Share of sales by meal period

Orders bucketed by time of day: Breakfast 6–11 AM · Lunch 11 AM–3 PM · Tea/Snacks 3–7 PM · Dinner after 7 PM.

Lunch is the business — almost half of all revenue (₹13.7L) and the richest basket (₹111 avg bill). Breakfast brings the most footfall but cheaper tickets. Dinner is under 5% — worth deciding whether it earns its keep.
04

Daily sales across the period

Bonus view — net sales each day. Orange bars are weekdays, green are weekends.

Is sales really falling? Not on volume — today's part-day is now excluded, and the daily rhythm holds steady at ₹70–80k on weekdays with regular weekend dips. Complete June 1–11 averaged ₹69.5k/day vs May's ₹66.2k (+5%, with more orders). The real soft spot is the lunch basket: the average lunch bill slipped ₹113 → ₹105 as customers traded down from premium chicken dishes — see the item movers in section 06.
05

Top sellers — what's on the tray

From the item-wise report (184 items over the period). Ranked by net sales. The kitchen is concentrated: the top 10 items make ~54% of all revenue.

Meals (the lunch thali) is the engine — ₹5.4L on its own (~20% of revenue, 9,065 plates), with Chicken Biryani next at ₹1.9L. Tea is third by value but #1 by volume (14,940 cups). Lunch revenue leans on Meals, Biryani and fish (Ayla / Nethal); breakfast is carried by Idly, Appam, Tea and Green Peas. Section 06 shows how these shifted May → June.
06

What moved: May → June

Change in net sales per day, complete May (2–31) vs complete June (1–11). The chart isolates exactly which dishes are behind the lunch softening — and what's quietly filling the gap.

It's a chicken story. Chicken Biryani fell ~25% (₹5,072 → ₹3,786/day, 30 → 22 plates) and Chicken Curry dropped ~21% — these high-ticket items are what pulled the lunch bill down. Meanwhile the ₹60 Meals thali held flat, and fish rose (Nethal Fry +21%, Ayla Curry +24%) alongside cheaper breakfast/snack items (Baji Curry, Poori, Kadala Curry, Bru Coffee). Customers are substituting away from chicken toward fish and cheaper plates — a likely sign of a chicken price, supply, or quality change in early June worth checking with the kitchen. Note too that half-biryani is up while full biryani is down: same dish, smaller basket.
