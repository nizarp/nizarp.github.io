06

Item demand heatmap

Quantities sold by hour for 10 key items. Darker = more sold. Reveals exactly when each item peaks — useful for prep scheduling.

Breakfast items (Idly, Appam, Idiyappam) peak hard at 7–9 AM then drop off sharply — prep cutoff can be around 11 AM. Meals is strictly a lunch item (12–2 PM). Tea runs all day with two surges (8–11 AM and 4–5 PM). Chapathi is the only item that keeps selling late into evening, making it a reliable dinner anchor.
07

Revenue leaders vs volume drivers

Two different angles on what matters most. Revenue leaders fund the canteen; volume drivers define the queue and kitchen load.

Meals is the single biggest revenue line at ₹4.13L — nearly 20% of all item revenue. The top 5 items together account for ~41% of revenue, so protecting their availability is critical.
Tea leads on volume at 11,485 units — but note its revenue rank is #3. The Parcel item is interesting: #4 by volume but low revenue (₹5/unit), suggesting it's a packaging/takeaway charge rather than food.
08

Hourly revenue & footfall — item report view

Total revenue and items sold each hour across the full period. Compares the value generated against the volume of items served — the divergence at 1 PM shows a high-value but relatively efficient lunch hour.

The 1 PM hour generated ₹4.25L in revenue on just ~8,000 item units — an avg item value of ₹53, driven by the premium Meals & Biryani products. Compare that to 8 AM: 11,629 items but only ₹1.71L — mostly low-ticket breakfast staples. Opportunity: nudge higher-value items (Ghee Rice, Chicken Biryani) slightly earlier into the 11 AM slot to capture pre-lunch appetite.
09

All-day menu anchors

Items that sold in 14 or more of the 16 trading hours — your most operationally critical items. If any of these run out, some part of the day's menu is always broken.

Chapathi, Kanji, Poricha Pathiri appear in all 16 hours. Tea is close with 15 hours. These items need deep prep stock from morning. Consider a par-level alert system — if any of these 10 items drops below a threshold before 2 PM, restock immediately.
10

Revenue concentration

How concentrated is the canteen's revenue? The top 5 items vs everything else.

Top 5 items = 40.8% of all revenue. That's healthy concentration — not dangerously over-reliant on one SKU, but enough that a supply issue with Meals or Chicken Biryani meaningfully hurts the day. Diversification into the mid-tier (Ayla Fry, Nethal Fry, Green Peas) is already underway.
Scaling lever: Chicken Biryani at ₹1.55L and only 917 units signals a ₹169/unit avg — the highest-value item after Ghee Rice. Even a 20% volume increase would add ~₹31k/month. Consider offering it from 11 AM instead of 12 PM to capture early-lunch demand.
