03

Share of sales by meal period

Orders bucketed by time of day: Breakfast 6–11 AM · Lunch 11 AM–3 PM · Tea/Snacks 3–7 PM · Dinner after 7 PM.

Lunch is the business — almost half of all revenue (₹10.6L) and the richest basket (₹109 avg bill). Breakfast brings the most footfall but cheaper tickets. Dinner is under 5% — worth deciding whether it earns its keep.
04

Daily sales across the period

Net sales each day (bars — orange = weekday, green = weekend) with the daily order count overlaid as a line, so you can see volume and revenue together.

Is sales really falling? Not on volume — with early-May and today's part-day removed, the daily rhythm holds steady at ₹70–80k on weekdays with regular weekend dips. June 1–11 averaged ₹69.5k/day vs the second half of May (12–31) at ₹67.9k (+2%). The real soft spot is the lunch basket: the average lunch bill slipped ₹112 → ₹105 as customers traded down from premium chicken dishes — see the item movers in section 06.
05

Top sellers — what's on the tray

From the item-wise report (170 items over the period). Ranked by net sales. The kitchen is concentrated: the top 10 items make ~54% of all revenue.

Meals (the lunch thali) is the engine — ₹4.2L on its own (~20% of revenue, 6,921 plates), with Chicken Biryani next at ₹1.4L. Tea is third by value but #1 by volume (11,272 cups). Lunch revenue leans on Meals, Biryani and fish (Ayla / Nethal); breakfast is carried by Idly, Appam, Tea and Green Peas. Section 06 shows how these shifted May → June.
06

What moved: May → June

Change in net sales per day, second-half May (12–31) vs June (1–11). The chart isolates exactly which dishes are behind the lunch softening — and what's quietly filling the gap.

It's a chicken story. Chicken Biryani fell ~25% (₹5,032 → ₹3,786/day) and Chicken Curry dropped ~16% — the high-ticket items that pulled the lunch bill down. The ₹60 Meals thali held roughly flat. Filling the gap: fish (Ayla Curry +22%) and cheaper plates (Baji Curry, Poori, Kadala Curry, Egg Puffs), plus half-biryani up while full biryani is down — same dish, smaller basket. Customers are substituting away from chicken, a likely sign of a chicken price, supply, or quality change in June worth checking with the kitchen.
07

Chicken Biryani & Chicken Curry, day by day

Net sales per day for the two declining chicken dishes (12 May – 11 Jun). Watch for whether the drop is a sudden cliff or a gradual slide.

No single cliff — it's a gradual level-shift. Both ran higher and steadier through the second half of May (Biryani averaging ₹5,032/day, Curry ₹2,076), then settled lower across June (₹3,786 and ₹1,745, down ~25% and ~16%) with weak weekend dips. Because there's no abrupt break on one date, the cause is more likely a standing change — a supply, price, or menu-availability shift around chicken — than a one-off event.
